Dog license and rabies tag rules for South Florida

Florida law requires a rabies vaccination for dogs. Miami-Dade, Broward and Palm Beach each add their own license or registration tag. A park can also set its own proof rule. This page separates those three layers. Fees below are the county prices published for a county tag or a county clinic, not a private veterinarian's price.

Official pages that do not agree
  • Miami-Dade, who can buy a tag. The license pages say you must be a Miami-Dade County resident. The vaccination requirements on the county pet vaccinations page say the dog must be registered in Miami-Dade, though the owner does not have to be a Miami-Dade resident. [Miami-Dade dog license, Miami-Dade animal licenses and tags, Miami-Dade pet vaccinations]
  • Miami-Dade, one-year or three-year tag. The dog license page says the tag must be renewed every year by the anniversary of the latest rabies vaccine, whether that vaccine lasts one year or three. The same page also says a three-year tag can be purchased when a three-year vaccine is given, that the tag expires with that vaccine, and that a three-year tag is renewed every three years. If you bought only a one-year tag, that page says you still renew every year. The animal licenses page says renewal is every year whether the vaccine lasts one year or three, and also says a three-year tag must be purchased when the three-year vaccine is given. [Miami-Dade dog license, Miami-Dade animal licenses and tags]
  • Miami-Dade, age wording. The license pages say a license is required for dogs over four months of age. The pet vaccinations page says dogs and cats must be four months old, that puppies need rabies vaccine when they turn 4 months old, and, in another answer on that page, that rabies vaccination is required for dogs, cats and ferrets over four months of age. [Miami-Dade dog license, Miami-Dade animal licenses and tags, Miami-Dade pet vaccinations]
  • Miami-Dade, clinic appointments. The pet vaccinations page says services are provided on a walk-in basis with no appointment, and the rabies vaccine answer says appointments are not required and service is provided as walk-ins. The heading of that same answer says rabies vaccines are now available by appointment only, and that the Wellness Clinic at the Animal Protection Center now operates by appointment only. [Miami-Dade pet vaccinations]
  • Broward, three-year registration. The fee list, marked updated June 30, 2026, includes a three-year tag: $65 if the pet is microchipped, or $145 if not, with a valid three-year rabies vaccine. Later on the same page, Broward says it does not offer a three-year registration renewal, and that a pet with a three-year vaccine still renews registration every year on the vaccination date. [Broward tags and shots]
  • Broward, rabies shot price. The clinic page lists the rabies vaccine as free at monthly wellness clinics, and the note under that list says the fees for the rabies vaccine and the registration tag are $25 for a microchipped pet or $55 if the pet is not microchipped. The Animal Care home page says the Pet Care Clinic is closed and is not taking appointments. [Broward pet rabies clinic, Broward Animal Care]
  • Palm Beach County, license age. The Animal Care rabies page says county ordinance requires every dog and cat 4 months and older to be current on rabies vaccine and the county license tag. County Code section 4-11 says the owner of a dog six months or older, or a cat four months or older, must get the rabies and license tag. Section 4-10 requires rabies vaccination in accordance with Florida Statutes section 828.30, which is 4 months of age or older. The county ordinance index lists the section titles and does not print the section text. [Palm Beach County rabies page, Palm Beach County Code chapter 4, Florida Statutes section 828.30, county ordinance index]
  • Palm Beach County, clinic hours. The Animal Care home page says a $15 rabies vaccine is offered with no appointment, on the hour and the half hour: Sunday 2:00 to 3:00 p.m., Monday 3:00 to 4:00 p.m., Tuesday through Friday 11:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., and Saturday 11:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. The rabies page says every Saturday and Sunday from 2 to 4 p.m., and that hours are subject to change or cancellation. [Palm Beach County Animal Care, Palm Beach County rabies page]

Statewide rabies rule

Florida Statutes section 828.30 says every dog, cat and ferret 4 months of age or older must be vaccinated against rabies with a United States Department of Agriculture licensed vaccine. The vaccination must be given by a licensed veterinarian. For an animal already in the custody of animal control or a sheriff, an employee, agent, or contractor may vaccinate under a veterinarian's indirect supervision. The owner pays. The first booster is due 12 months after the first shot. After that, the interval follows the vaccine maker's directions. A blood test for rabies antibodies does not replace a current vaccination. The veterinarian must give the owner and the animal-control authority a rabies vaccination certificate. A veterinarian can exempt an animal in writing if vaccination would endanger its health because of age, infirmity, disability, illness, or other medical reasons, and the animal must be vaccinated when its health allows. A violation is a civil infraction. The statute does not set a county license fee, and it allows counties and cities to be stricter. It also says a local government cannot order another vaccination of an animal that is already currently vaccinated, except for rabies exposure treatment. [Florida Statutes section 828.30]

Miami-Dade County

The county dog license page says a dog license is required for every dog in Miami-Dade County over four months of age. The animal licenses page says the dog must be registered and must wear the tag at all times. You need proof of a current rabies vaccination. The county says you cannot buy or renew the tag without that proof. The person registering the dog must be 18. The license pages also say the person must be a Miami-Dade resident, with a driver's license or passport, and the licenses page adds proof of a Miami-Dade address. See the conflict above about whether a non-resident's dog can be registered. [Miami-Dade dog license, Miami-Dade animal licenses and tags, Miami-Dade pet vaccinations]

A cat license is available and is not required. Cats still have to be vaccinated against rabies. [Miami-Dade animal licenses and tags]

Tag cost

  • One-year, spayed or neutered: $30. Proof of spay or neuter is required.
  • One-year, not spayed or neutered: $60.
  • One-year, dog under 12 months: $30, listed under both altered and unaltered dogs.
  • Three-year, spayed or neutered: $90. Three-year, not spayed or neutered: $180. The three-year tag is tied to a three-year vaccine. See the renewal conflict above.
  • One-year low-income: $5 if spayed or neutered, $15 if not. The county asks for a current eligibility letter for food stamps, Medicaid, Supplemental Security Income, a Jackson Memorial Hospital clinic card, or Temporary Assistance for Needed Families (the county's spelling).
  • Replacement tag: $5. The new tag keeps the old expiration date.
  • Service-animal tag: free at the Pet Adoption and Protection Center. The county says the dog must be trained to do work or a task directly related to a person's disability, that a verbal statement of that task is enough, and that emotional support, therapy, comfort, and companion animals do not qualify. Residency and rabies proof still apply.
  • Police-dog tag: free. The handler still meets the vaccination and licensing rules, and the rabies certificate must show the police station or city government address.

The pet vaccinations page says there is a 30-day grace period from the license and rabies expiration date instead of a late fee. After that, the county says it issues a civil violation notice. A veterinarian can exempt a dog from the rabies vaccine for up to one year for health reasons. The license tag is still required. The written exemption goes to Animal Services within 30 days of the vaccine expiration, by email to petacctupdates@miamidade.gov, by mail, in person at the Pet Adoption and Protection Center with a Wellness Clinic appointment, or at a 311 service center. The same answer says dogs that stay in Miami-Dade for less than 30 days are exempt from that vaccination requirement. [Miami-Dade pet vaccinations]

Where to get the tag and the shot

The county says you can buy or renew a dog tag at the Pet Adoption and Protection Center, a local veterinarian, a 311 service center, or by mail. The licenses page also says tags can be purchased and renewed online, and it names the Humane Society of Greater Miami and the American Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Liberty City location. Mail payment is by check or money order only, with a copy of the rabies certificate, payable to Miami-Dade Animal Services Tag Unit, 3599 Northwest 79 Avenue, Doral, Florida 33122. The 311 service centers take a personal check, cashier's check, or money order. [Miami-Dade dog license, Miami-Dade animal licenses and tags]

Tag counter at the Pet Adoption and Protection Center, 3599 Northwest 79 Avenue, Doral: Monday through Friday 10:00 a.m. to 6:30 p.m., Saturday and Sunday 8:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. Closed county holidays. Saffir Permitting and Inspection Center, 11805 Southwest 26 Street, Miami, second-floor kiosk, same weekday hours; North Dade Regional Library, 2455 Northwest 183rd Street, Miami Gardens, Monday through Friday 9:30 a.m. to 5:30 p.m., closed noon to 1:00 p.m. [Miami-Dade dog license]

The pet vaccinations page lists Wellness Clinic hours in the same Doral building: Monday through Friday 9:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m., Saturday and Sunday 9:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. Closed county holidays. See the appointment conflict above before you rely on walk-in service. Dogs and cats must be four months old. An individual rabies vaccine is $25. The low-income rabies vaccine is $10 with the eligibility letter described above. Those are county clinic prices. Broward County

Broward County says every dog, cat and ferret must receive a rabies vaccination, and that every dog and cat must have and wear a Broward County pet rabies registration tag. Dogs and cats four months or older must be registered. Indoor pets are not exempt. A tattoo, microchip, or name tag does not replace the county tag. The tag is a lifetime tag that you buy once. Registration still has to be renewed. A current rabies certificate from a licensed veterinarian is required at the first purchase and at renewal. If a veterinarian waives the vaccine for health reasons, the tag is still required. The tags page does not describe a short-visit exception. [Broward tags and shots]

Registration is valid for one year from the date of the rabies vaccination, not from the day you buy the tag. The page says to renew within 30 days of the vaccination, and that a late fee applies if the tag or renewal is bought more than 30 days after vaccination or more than 30 days after the registration expires. It says fines for no vaccination or no registration are issued after 60 days. The stated fine is $300 for no rabies vaccination and $300 for no county registration. The late fee is $10. You are not in violation if the dog has a current rabies vaccine, you have paid for the tag, and you are waiting for it in the mail. [Broward tags and shots]

Tag cost

The fee depends on a microchip, not on whether the dog is spayed or neutered. The fee section was marked updated June 30, 2026.

  • Microchipped dog or cat: $25 for one year. The page also lists $65 for three years with a valid three-year rabies vaccine. See the conflict above. The county says it does not offer a three-year renewal.
  • Not microchipped: $55 for one year, and the page also lists $145 for three years with a valid three-year vaccine.
  • Replacement tag: $10, and only at the Adoption Center.
  • Online or veterinary-office purchase: a service fee of not more than $5 per tag. The tags page does not add that service fee to a tag bought at the Adoption Center.

Where to get the tag and the shot

Buy the tag at the Adoption Center, at an approved veterinary office, online (upload the rabies certificate; the tag is not issued until the county receives it), or by mail. Tag sales at the Adoption Center are Monday through Sunday, 11:00 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. Bring a copy of the rabies certificate. Animal Care is at 2400 Southwest 42nd Street, Fort Lauderdale, Florida 33312. Mail a check or money order payable to the Board of County Commissioners, Animal Care Division, to Broward County Animal Licensing, in care of PetData, Post Office Box 141929, Irving, Texas 75014-1929. [Broward tags and shots, Broward Animal Care]

The county has not published a single rabies-shot price. See the clinic conflict above. The on-site Pet Care Clinic is temporarily closed and is not taking new appointments. The clinic page's listed mobile clinic date, Wednesday, October 14, 2026, was already past on this check. The page says to check back for the next date. Pets must be 4 months or older for a rabies vaccine at those clinics. The contact printed for clinic registration is petcareclinic@broward.org or 954-357-9567. A private veterinarian can give the vaccine. This page does not have a county price for that visit. [Broward pet rabies clinic, Broward Animal Care]

Palm Beach County

County Code chapter 4 applies in both cities and unincorporated areas unless a municipal exemption applies. This check did not find a published exemption for Boca Raton or Jupiter. The county's ordinance index does not list exempt cities. [Palm Beach County Code chapter 4, county ordinance index]

Use the age conflict above before you rely on a single number. Section 4-10 requires rabies vaccination in line with the state statute. A visitor who is in the county for 30 days or less meets that rabies section with a current certificate from home. A dog or cat without one must be vaccinated and given a county certificate. A dog or cat that moves in for more than 30 days needs a current certificate that matches the county form, or a new county vaccination and certificate. [Palm Beach County Code chapter 4, Florida Statutes section 828.30]

Section 4-11 says no rabies and license tag is issued until a licensed veterinarian's rabies proof is shown. The tag is valid for one year from the vaccination date, must be renewed every year, and cannot last past the vaccine expiration. Even with a three-year vaccine, the tag is never effective for more than one year. The effective dates for the later years are one year and two years after the vaccination date. Buy the tag by the schedule in the code: by the age in section 4-11, within 30 days of getting the dog, or within 30 days after the dog enters the county. Missing the renewal by more than 30 days after the old tag expires brings a late penalty. The code says the County Commission sets that penalty by resolution. The Animal Care fee list does not state the late-penalty amount. [Palm Beach County Code chapter 4, Palm Beach County rabies page]

Every dog must wear a valid county license tag, fastened so it is clearly visible. A dog in a secure enclosure does not have to wear it if the tag is fastened to a collar or harness that is attached to the enclosure. Dogs do not wear it during a registered field trial, obedience trial, conformation show, or match. [Palm Beach County Code chapter 4]
